# C2PA Provenance and NTIA Obligations for GenAI

## Motivation

Chapters `01`–`04` walked the systemic-risk-provider obligations, the frontier-framework shape, the evaluator envelope, and the safety-benchmark citation pack. What remains for a complete GPAI-systemic-risk assurance package is the *content-provenance* and *dual-use-openness* overlay — the two threads that sit on top of the core Article 55 discharge and address, respectively, "how does the world know what this system produced?" and "what changes when the model's weights are widely available?"

Both threads are already partly covered elsewhere in the track. `mod-105` chapter `06` teaches C2PA in depth from the card-authoring angle — how the manifest attaches to a specific asset, how the trust-list root of trust works, what disclosure position the manifest carries, and how the redaction manifest handles disclosure-secrecy trade-offs. This chapter does *not* re-teach that depth. It reads C2PA from the *GPAI-assurance* angle: the manifest is *evidence the assurance case cites*, and the trust-list membership is *itself a governance decision*.

The dual-use openness thread is new for this module. The [NTIA Report on Dual-Use Foundation Models with Widely Available Model Weights](https://www.ntia.gov/programs-and-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/report-on-dual-use-foundation-models), published 2024-07 in response to Executive Order 14110, is the reference US-government analysis of the trade-offs open-weights foundation models introduce. Its findings inform the disclosure and openness sections of a GPAI-systemic-risk assurance package — even when the release is not itself open-weights, because the report frames the trade-off shape the assurance package has to reason about.

## C2PA in one paragraph — the GPAI-assurance reading

The [C2PA technical specification](https://c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/) (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) is an open standard for cryptographically signed content-provenance manifests attached to digital assets. A manifest carries **assertions** (things claimed about the asset), a **hard binding** to the asset's bytes (a hash), and a **signature** by a producer whose credential chains to a root of trust maintained via the C2PA **trust list**. Assertions may include the tool that produced the asset, the AI-generated status, the training-data provenance category, the actions applied since generation (crop, edit, colour-correct), and any human-authored steps. Manifests can be **chained** — an edited asset carries the manifest for the original generation plus manifests for each subsequent transformation, walkable end-to-end. Manifests can be **redacted** — parts of an assertion can be selectively withheld while preserving the signature validity on the rest.

For the GPAI-assurance package, four properties matter:

1. **Manifests are opt-in and cryptographic — not universal proof.** C2PA does not prove content was generated by a specific model; it proves a *credentialed producer* attested to the content's provenance at signing time. Missing manifests do not prove non-provenance; present manifests do prove the specific producer's attestation.
2. **Manifests are content-addressable and signed.** A C2PA manifest for a produced asset is itself a store artefact (`mod-104` chapter `01`), with its own digest, and the manifest signature verifies against the producer credential chain.
3. **The trust list is a governance surface.** Whose signing credentials are on the trust list determines whose manifests are meaningful. Trust-list membership decisions carry governance weight and are documented in the assurance case.
4. **The manifest chains for edits and derivations.** An assurance case that ships a GenAI system whose outputs will be edited downstream (or that consumes upstream content with C2PA manifests) has to reason about *chained* manifests, not just leaf ones.

## Where C2PA lands in the GPAI-assurance package

### As evidence for Article 50 marking-and-labelling obligations

EU AI Act Article 50 imposes transparency obligations on providers and deployers of certain AI systems, including specific rules for generative AI. Article 50(2) requires providers of AI systems (including GPAI systems) generating synthetic audio, image, video, or text content to ensure that outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. Article 50(4) requires deployers of AI systems generating or manipulating image, audio, or video content constituting a deep fake to disclose that fact (with narrow exceptions).

**What it says.** Article 50(2) requires machine-readable marking of synthetic outputs. Article 50(4) requires deployer-side disclosure of deep-fake content.

**Release-assurance implication.** The assurance case carries, per GenAI-output-producing system, a *provenance-and-disclosure section* citing the C2PA manifest schema, the producer credential chain, the specific assertions the manifest includes, and the disclosure-position rationale for Article 50(2) and 50(4). `mod-105` chapter `06` gives the deep shape of the `provenance.c2pa` block on the assurance card; the GPAI assurance case cites into that block.

### As a chained-evidence surface for upstream and downstream provenance

A systemic-risk GPAI is upstream of many downstream products. If the GPAI's outputs are C2PA-signed by the provider, downstream deployers can preserve the chain (attaching their own manifests for post-generation transformations) or break it (stripping the manifest). The assurance case carries:

- **Signing infrastructure** — the signing service that produces manifests at generation time; its keys; its OIDC identity; its Fulcio-issued cert; its audit log.
- **Chain-preservation contract** — the terms in the downstream-provider integration documentation (chapter `01`'s Model Documentation Form, from the Code of Practice transparency chapter) that require or recommend chain preservation.
- **Verification endpoint** — where a consumer can verify the manifest chain for an asset produced by the system.
- **Trust-list membership position** — whose credentials the assurance programme accepts as trust-list-member producers for chained-manifest verification.

### As a redaction-aware evidence node

C2PA manifests support *redaction* — an assertion can be selectively withheld while preserving signature validity on the rest of the manifest. This matters for GPAI-assurance because a manifest may carry assertions that are appropriate for one audience (regulator, third-party evaluator) but not another (general public). The redaction-manifest discipline `mod-105` chapter `06` teaches applies inside the C2PA manifest layer as well: the assurance case names which assertions are redacted per audience variant and cites the redaction rationale.

### As a governance surface — trust-list membership

The C2PA trust list is a *governance decision*, not a technical one. Whose signing credentials are on the trust list, who maintains the list, what the accession criteria are, and what the revocation procedure is — all of that is governance-surface content the assurance case documents. A GPAI-systemic-risk provider on the trust list *is* a governance decision by whoever maintains the list; a GPAI-systemic-risk provider consuming trust-list-verified content is making a governance decision to accept that root of trust.

**Release-assurance implication.** The assurance case's C2PA section names:

- Which trust list(s) the provider is a member of (Content Authenticity Initiative, C2PA's own, industry-specific).
- Which trust list(s) the provider's own verification service consults when checking upstream manifests.
- The revocation procedure — how the provider handles a trust-list member whose credential is later revoked, and how downstream consumers of the provider's manifests handle revocation of the provider's own credential.
- The escalation procedure — who at the provider is authorised to *change* the trust-list position (add a new list, remove one, treat a specific member's assertions as untrusted following an incident).

### As a complement — not a substitute — to watermarking

For text outputs (and increasingly for other modalities), statistical watermarking (e.g., DeepMind's SynthID for text) provides a separate provenance signal that survives content transformations that strip C2PA manifests. `mod-105` chapter `06` teaches the C2PA-and-watermarking-are-complementary discipline in depth; from the GPAI-assurance angle, the assurance case's provenance section carries *both* the C2PA position and the watermarking position when the modality supports both, with the reader knowing that C2PA is cryptographic but strippable and watermarking is statistical but harder to strip.

## NTIA Report on Dual-Use Foundation Models with Widely Available Model Weights

### What the report is

The [NTIA Report on Dual-Use Foundation Models with Widely Available Model Weights](https://www.ntia.gov/programs-and-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/report-on-dual-use-foundation-models), published 2024-07, was produced by the National Telecommunications and Information Administration in response to Executive Order 14110 (2023-10, subsequently revoked in 2025 but with continuing legacy effect on documents like this one). The report analyses the risks and benefits of foundation models whose weights are widely available (open-weights or open-release models), makes recommendations for the US government's approach, and — significantly — declines to recommend restrictions on the availability of model weights at the time of publication, while recommending monitoring and evidence-gathering.

### What the report says at citation depth

- **Framing.** The report distinguishes *widely available model weights* (open-weights, downloadable, replicable) from *closed-weight* models (accessible only via API). The trade-off analysis applies to models with widely available weights.
- **Marginal risk framing.** The report evaluates risks in terms of *marginal risk* — the additional risk introduced by open-weights availability, beyond the counterfactual risk from closed-weight availability or other means. This framing matters: it does not treat open-weights as maximally risky, and it does not treat them as risk-neutral.
- **Recommendations at time of publication.** The report recommends the US government actively monitor the risks and benefits of open-weights foundation models, gather evidence, engage with the developer community, and *not* impose restrictions on model-weight availability at that time.
- **Ongoing analysis.** The report frames a continuing analytical programme rather than a one-time verdict; the marginal-risk assessment can change as evidence accumulates.

### How the report informs the GPAI-assurance package's disclosure and openness sections

Even a GPAI-systemic-risk provider releasing under a *closed-weight* model has to reason about openness — because the assurance case's disclosure section has to state where on the openness spectrum the release sits, why, and how that position discharges the applicable obligations. The NTIA report gives the framing.

**For a closed-weight release.** The assurance case cites the NTIA marginal-risk framing to justify why a closed-weight release does not create the same disclosure obligations that an open-weight release would. Article 55(1)(b) systemic-risk mitigation for a closed-weight release can rely on access-control mitigations (API rate-limiting, use-case restrictions in the Terms of Service, monitoring) that are not available for an open-weight release.

**For an open-weight release.** The assurance case cites the NTIA report as the reference analysis of the trade-offs, states which recommendations the provider has adopted (monitoring commitments, evidence-gathering, developer-community engagement), and cites the specific mitigations that address the residual risks (pre-release evaluation depth, gradual-release strategy, post-release monitoring, developer-community coordination).

**For a partial-openness release** (weights released with restrictions, gated access, staged release across regions or tiers). The assurance case names the specific openness position, cites the NTIA framing for the reasoning, and documents the mitigations that address the openness-specific residual risks.

### The three positions on the openness spectrum

Concretely, a GPAI-systemic-risk release lands in one of three broad openness positions, each with its own assurance-case discharge shape:

- **Closed-weight.** Weights are held internally; access is via API or hosted product. Mitigations rely on access controls (authentication, rate limiting, TOS-based use-case restrictions, monitoring, kill-switch). The provider retains ability to revoke access, rate-limit specific users, or patch model behaviour centrally.
- **Open-weight.** Weights are published (typically on Hugging Face, GitHub, or a similar host) under an open licence, downloadable by anyone. Mitigations must be intrinsic to the weights themselves (unlearning of hazardous knowledge, refusal training, or capability-limitation choices at training time), or must operate at the ecosystem level (developer-community coordination, incident reporting from downstream integrators, threat monitoring for misuse).
- **Gated-open / partial-openness.** Weights are available under specific access terms (research access, application-based release, staged geographic release, weight release with agreed monitoring). This position blends the two shapes: some access control remains, some intrinsic mitigations are still required.

The three positions are not equivalent for systemic-risk mitigation purposes. Article 55(1)(b) requires *mitigation of systemic risks at Union level*; the mitigation set available in each openness position is different, and the assurance case has to name which set applies.

### Interaction with Article 55(1)(b) Union-level systemic-risk assessment

The Union-level systemic-risk assessment (Article 55(1)(b), chapter `01`) includes assessment of risks that stem from the *placing on the market* of the GPAI model. Openness position is a placing-on-the-market decision; the NTIA report is the current reference analysis of what that decision implies for risk. A Union-level assessment that does not reason about openness is under-serving the article; a Union-level assessment that reasons about openness without citing the NTIA framing (or an equivalent EU-side analysis, where one exists) is missing the reference.

## Worked example — content-provenance-and-openness section of a systemic-risk GPAI

Continuing the worked example from chapters `01`–`04`, the assurance bundle's content-provenance-and-openness section reads (excerpt):

- **Provenance layer.** The GenAI system's output-generation service produces C2PA-signed outputs for image, video, and audio modalities (text output provenance uses a complementary mechanism per `mod-105` chapter `06`'s adjacent-mechanism section). The C2PA producer credential is issued by the enterprise's internal CA, whose root is a member of the C2PA trust list. Each manifest carries the `c2pa.ai-generated` assertion, the `c2pa.actions` assertion, and — where applicable — the `c2pa.training-mining-and-generative-ai-use` assertion. The verification endpoint is `https://provenance.example.corp/verify`.
- **Article 50 discharge.** The provenance layer discharges Article 50(2) (machine-readable marking). Downstream deployer contracts include the Article 50(4) deep-fake-disclosure obligation as a pass-through requirement, with the enterprise's tool-UI disclosure as the default implementation for direct deployments.
- **Trust-list governance.** The enterprise's CA is on the C2PA trust list under accession terms recorded in the `provenance.c2pa.trust_list_governance` block of the assurance card (`mod-105` chapter `06`). The revocation procedure is documented in the same block.
- **Openness position.** The GPAI model is closed-weight, released via API only. The assurance case's openness section cites the NTIA report's marginal-risk framing to justify the closed-weight choice, names the access-control mitigations in place (API authentication, rate limiting, use-case restrictions in the Terms of Service, monitoring for prohibited use, kill-switch), and interlocks with the Article 55(1)(b) Union-level systemic-risk assessment.
- **Openness-monitoring commitment.** The provider commits to periodic re-assessment of the openness position (aligned with the frontier framework's re-evaluation cadence from chapter `02`) — the openness position is not a one-time decision.

## Anti-patterns

- **C2PA-without-a-trust-list-position.** Producing C2PA-signed outputs without documenting which trust list(s) the producer's credential is registered under, and without a revocation procedure. A C2PA manifest whose credential chains nowhere is not evidence.
- **Openness-as-a-checkbox.** Naming the openness position ("closed-weight", "open-weight", "gated-open") without citing the NTIA framing (or equivalent) and without linking to the mitigations that discharge the openness-specific residual risks. Openness is not a checkbox; it is a governance decision requiring justification.
- **Manifest-in-transit-only.** Shipping C2PA manifests on the wire but not persisting them in the evidence store as content-addressed artefacts. Six months later, the assurance case cannot walk to the manifest a downstream consumer received.
- **Chain-preservation-without-contract.** Recommending that downstream deployers preserve the C2PA chain without a contractual mechanism (in the Model Documentation Form's downstream-provider terms, chapter `01`) requiring it. Chain preservation is a *cooperative* property; the contract is what makes it enforceable.
- **NTIA-report-as-permission-slip.** Citing the NTIA report's decline-to-recommend-restrictions as if it were a permission slip for any openness position. The report frames a *marginal-risk analysis*; the assurance case has to *do* the analysis for the specific release, not just cite the report.
- **Watermarking-without-detector.** Advertising a watermarking scheme alongside C2PA without publishing (or making available under regulatory access) the detection endpoint reduces the scheme to marketing — the same anti-pattern `mod-105` chapter `06` names, restated here because it is easy to reintroduce at the GPAI-assurance layer even when the card layer got it right.
- **Openness-decision-without-post-market-loop.** Naming an openness position at release without connecting it to `mod-110`'s post-market surveillance loop. Openness position is not release-static; monitoring signals from downstream deployers and from the developer community feed back into re-assessment. A closed-weight release that ships without a post-market re-assessment cadence is under-serving Article 55(1)(b) just as much as an open-weight release without one.
- **Silent openness change.** Changing the openness position between releases without documenting the change in the assurance bundle's version history and without re-running the systemic-risk assessment for the new position. Openness is a first-class release parameter; changes are release-triggering events.

## Summary

- The GPAI-systemic-risk assurance package's content-provenance overlay cites [C2PA](https://c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/) as the machine-readable-marking mechanism for Article 50(2) discharge, with the producer credential chain, the trust-list membership position, and the redaction-aware manifest discipline documented.
- C2PA manifests are content-addressed evidence nodes in the assurance store; the trust list is a governance surface with membership and revocation decisions documented in the assurance case. `mod-105` chapter `06` owns the depth; this chapter reads from the GPAI-assurance side.
- The [NTIA Report on Dual-Use Foundation Models with Widely Available Model Weights](https://www.ntia.gov/programs-and-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/report-on-dual-use-foundation-models) (2024-07) frames the marginal-risk analysis for open-weights foundation-model releases and informs the disclosure and openness sections of the assurance package, whether the release is open-weights, closed-weight, or partial-openness.
- The Union-level systemic-risk assessment (Article 55(1)(b)) includes openness-position reasoning; the NTIA framing is the current reference analysis, with EU-side analyses to be cited where available.
- Anti-patterns to avoid: C2PA-without-trust-list-position, openness-as-a-checkbox, manifest-in-transit-only, chain-preservation-without-contract, NTIA-report-as-permission-slip.
