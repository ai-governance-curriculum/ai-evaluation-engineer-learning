# C2PA Provenance for GenAI, and the Disclosure-Secrecy Trade-off

## Motivation

The card sections so far have carried claims *about* the system. This chapter adds two threads the assurance card has to weave in for any GenAI-facing product:

- **Content provenance for the system's outputs.** A generative system produces artefacts (images, video, audio, text, documents) that leave the system boundary and circulate. Under the EU AI Act Article 50 (transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems, including specific rules for generative AI and deepfakes), under the [White House Executive Order 14110 (revoked 2025)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/11/01/2023-24283/safe-secure-and-trustworthy-development-and-use-of-artificial-intelligence) legacy tooling still shipping, and under many voluntary commitments (the [Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity, C2PA](https://c2pa.org/), the [Content Authenticity Initiative, CAI](https://contentauthenticity.org/)), a card's obligations extend to *provenance for what the system produces*, not only claims about the system itself.
- **Disclosure discipline.** The card has genuinely conflicting obligations. A public disclosure has to be maximally transparent for accountability. But the same card cannot publish attack payloads that walk a reader through a jailbreak, cannot publish PII in eval-set examples, cannot publish canary tokens whose secrecy is what makes contamination detection work (mod-104 chapter `05`). Chapter `07` names the four audience variants; this chapter names the *fields* the redaction discipline operates on and the reasoning a card author uses to decide what to withhold.

## C2PA in one paragraph

The [C2PA technical specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/) is an open standard for cryptographically signed content-provenance manifests attached to digital assets. Adobe, Microsoft, BBC, Intel, Truepic, and others founded the coalition; the [Joint Development Foundation](https://www.jointdevelopment.org/) hosts it. A C2PA manifest is a signed JSON structure that carries **assertions** (things claimed about the asset), a **hash-binding** to the asset's bytes, and a **signature** by a producer (or a chain of producers) whose credential chains to a root of trust. Assertions can name the tool that produced the asset, the training data disclosure category (per the [C2PA training-data assertion vocabulary](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.1/)), the AI-generated status, the actions applied since generation (crop, edit, colour-correct), and any human-authored steps. The [Content Credentials](https://contentcredentials.org/) initiative operationalises the specification for public tooling.

Two properties of C2PA that matter for the card:

- **Manifests are chained.** An image edited after generation carries a manifest for the original generation *and* a manifest for the edit; the chain is walkable. A card that ships a C2PA-signed output carries the manifest schema and the producer credential; a card that consumes upstream C2PA (RAG over web content, for example) can also cite what it *did not sign*.
- **Manifests are opt-in and cryptographic — not universal proof.** C2PA does not prove that content was generated by a specific model. It proves that a specific *credentialed producer* attested to the content's provenance at the time of signing. Removing or stripping the manifest is trivial; the presence of the manifest is what confers trust, not its absence.

## The C2PA manifest on the assurance card

The assurance card carries, for every GenAI-output-producing system, a `provenance.c2pa` block:

```yaml
provenance:
  c2pa:
    manifest_content_address: "sha256:d4e0..."       # the manifest itself as an artefact in the store
    manifest_generator: "example.corp/genai-signer/v1.4"
    producer_credential:
      issuer: "example.corp CA"
      subject: "internal-assistant/v2026-04-11"
      credential_content_address: "sha256:a712..."
    assertions:
      - kind: "c2pa.actions"
        content_address: "sha256:b834..."
      - kind: "c2pa.training-mining-and-generative-ai-use"
        content_address: "sha256:c945..."
      - kind: "c2pa.ai-generated"                    # or "not-ai-generated" for downstream tools
        content_address: "sha256:d056..."
    disclosure_position:                             # relative to EU AI Act Article 50
      article_50_1: "no-real-person-deception"       # narrative rationale
      article_50_2_ai_generated_watermarking: true
      article_50_3_deepfake_disclosure: "disclosed-in-tool-output"
      article_50_4_public_interest_text: "not-in-scope"  # or "in-scope-with-disclosure"
    verification_endpoint: "https://provenance.example.corp/verify"
    fallback_declaration:                            # if manifest missing / stripped
      content_address: "sha256:5e67..."
```

Three invariants over the block:

- **The manifest is itself content-addressed in the store.** A C2PA manifest that is not in the evidence store cannot be walked by a reviewer six months later. The manifest is signed by the producer's credential; the credential chain is also stored.
- **The `disclosure_position` reads to the specific Article 50 sub-obligations.** A card that says "we do C2PA" without naming which sub-obligation it discharges is under-serving the Article 50 reviewer (deep coverage in mod-106).
- **A fallback declaration is present.** C2PA manifests can be stripped in transit; the card carries a fallback textual disclosure (chapter `07` returns to how this shows up in the tool UI and in the deployer contract).

For image / video / audio, the manifest is the standard C2PA manifest chained to the asset's bytes. For text (which C2PA extends more recently under the [text assertion work](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.1/)), the manifest is chained to a canonical form of the text. For document outputs (PDFs, DOCX) the C2PA specification's document format assertions cover the pattern.

## Adjacent provenance mechanisms worth naming

The card block above is C2PA-shaped, but the ecosystem carries three neighbours a card author has to be able to place.

- **Statistical / cryptographic watermarking (e.g., Google DeepMind's SynthID for text, image, audio; academic work on token-level watermarking).** These do not require a signed manifest attached to the artefact; the signal is inside the artefact's bytes and is detectable by a verifier who knows the scheme. A card that ships a watermarking scheme carries the scheme identifier and the detection-endpoint as a *separate* provenance field, not inside the C2PA block. Watermarking and C2PA are complementary: C2PA is opt-in and strippable but strongly verifiable when present; watermarking is opt-in-by-generator and harder to strip but statistical rather than cryptographic. The card can and often should carry both.
- **Model output identifiers (returned in the API).** For text APIs, the generation response often carries an ID that lets a downstream consumer query "was this output generated by our API." The card's `verification_endpoint` binds this if the vendor exposes one.
- **[IPTC](https://iptc.org/) photo metadata and its `digitalSourceType` field for AI-generated imagery.** This is upstream of C2PA and is what a stripped-manifest fallback often looks like; the card's `fallback_declaration` may cite the IPTC schema.

## The disclosure-vs-secrecy trade-off

The rest of the chapter names the fields that *most often* raise disclosure-secrecy tension on a card, and the reasoning a card author uses to decide what to withhold in each audience variant. Chapter `07` operationalises the decisions into a `redaction_manifest`.

### 1. Attack payloads

**The tension.** Chapter `03` §5 and chapter `05`'s robustness row report an attack-success-rate metric — say, 3.1% jailbreak success on a red-team set. To be *walkable*, the metric's eval-report has to name the attack set. But naming the attack set with actual payload text hands a would-be attacker a menu. Frontier labs have converged on a discipline: report the rate, name the attack *category* (per taxonomy — MITRE ATLAS, OWASP LLM Top 10, an internal taxonomy), and withhold the specific payloads from the public variant.

**Decision rule.**
- **Public variant.** Report the rate, the category, the taxonomy row, and the trend against previous versions. Withhold the payloads themselves. Reference the taxonomy publicly.
- **Regulator variant.** Report the rate, the category, and — under confidentiality provisions of the regulatory regime — the payloads on request.
- **Third-party evaluator variant.** Report all of the above under the evaluation agreement (mod-109), including the payloads and the reproducibility bundle. The evaluator is bound by a not-to-leak clause.
- **Board variant.** Report the rate against threshold, the trend, and the residual-risk narrative. The payloads are not what the board needs; the trend and the escalation position are.

**The rationale.** The public interest in accountability is served by the rate + taxonomy + trend. The public interest in *not being handed a jailbreak menu* is served by withholding the payloads. This is a specific application of the general operational-security exemption; the card's `redaction_manifest` names the exemption cited (see chapter `07`).

### 2. PII in eval sets

**The tension.** Eval sets often contain real user prompts, real names, real addresses — or the eval set is *derived from* real production traffic and preserves personal characteristics. Publishing the eval set means publishing the PII; not publishing means the reviewer cannot walk the reproducibility bundle.

**Decision rule.**
- **Public variant.** Publish the *eval-set metadata* (size, composition, coverage of demographic categories, licensing) and the eval-set integrity attestation (mod-104 chapter `05`). Withhold the raw eval set. Publish a *derived* eval set with synthetic PII (or a re-consented sample) as a separate content-addressed dataset if the program supports one.
- **Regulator variant.** The eval set is available under the regulator's data-access authority (EU AI Act Article 74; Article 60 for market-surveillance authorities; national supervisory authority under GDPR data-access provisions).
- **Third-party evaluator variant.** The eval set is available under a data-processing agreement that limits access to the evaluator, prohibits re-identification, and mandates return-and-destroy at engagement close.
- **Board variant.** The eval-set metadata is enough.

**The rationale.** Publishing PII in an eval set is a GDPR / CCPA / sectoral-privacy-law breach; withholding it entirely breaks reproducibility. The compromise is a derived synthetic eval set for public reproduction plus real-set access under legal frames for regulators and evaluators.

### 3. Decontamination canaries and eval-set contamination fingerprints

**The tension.** Mod-104 chapter `05` describes canary tokens and statistical fingerprints for detecting whether a public benchmark has been trained on. The technique only works if the canaries and fingerprints stay secret; publishing them nullifies the detection mechanism. But the reviewer needs to know that decontamination was performed, at what tolerance, and how it was checked.

**Decision rule.**
- **Public variant.** Publish the decontamination methodology at the *category* level (n-gram overlap tests, semantic-similarity thresholds, canary-token spot-checks) and the *attestation* that the tests were performed with the resulting rates. Withhold the specific canary tokens, the fingerprint construction, and the exact tolerance thresholds. The attestation is *statistical* — the card publishes an aggregate number (e.g., 0.02% suspected contamination at rejection floor, with methodology cited) and the aggregate is under a redaction commitment that reveals only the aggregate.
- **Regulator variant.** The methodology and (under confidentiality) the specific canary tokens and thresholds are available.
- **Third-party evaluator variant.** Under mod-109's evaluation-agreement discipline, the evaluator receives the canaries and reruns the detection independently on the released bundle. This is the strongest form of the attestation.
- **Board variant.** The aggregate rate and the escalation position are what the board needs.

**The rationale.** Canaries and fingerprints are protection mechanisms whose value depends on secrecy. Statistical redaction — reporting the aggregate outcome while withholding the mechanism — preserves both accountability and the mechanism.

### 4. Trade-secret / IP-protected training-data disclosures

**The tension.** Full training-data disclosure names licensed corpora, third-party providers, and internal collections that may carry contractual restrictions and IP protection. Full non-disclosure leaves the Article 10 (EU AI Act) and Article 53 (GPAI training-data summary) obligations under-served.

**Decision rule.**
- **Public variant.** Publish the training-data disclosure at the level Article 53 requires (a "sufficiently detailed summary about the content used for training") — data categories, provenance categories, licensing categories, temporal cutoffs. Withhold specific supplier names where trade-secret protection applies.
- **Regulator variant.** Under Article 74's confidentiality provisions and the regulator's own information-security discipline, the specific suppliers are available.
- **Third-party evaluator variant.** Depends on the engagement scope; typically at the same level as the regulator variant with an evaluation agreement.
- **Board variant.** The categories and the coverage rate are what the board needs.

**The rationale.** Public disclosure serves accountability; trade-secret protection serves competitive fairness and supplier contract compliance. The Article 53 summary is a designed compromise; the card cites it.

### 5. Vulnerability windows and unpatched failure modes

**The tension.** A safety-evidence summary that names an unmitigated failure mode (e.g., "the guardrail fails on prompts of the form X because Y") is Chekhov's gun for adversaries. But omitting it while the failure exists is misrepresentation.

**Decision rule.**
- **Public variant.** Name the failure category (at taxonomy level), the mitigation-in-progress, the deployment-scope restriction (if any), and the escalation position (in mod-110's post-market monitoring). Withhold the specific reproduction.
- **Regulator variant.** Publish the specific reproduction under the regulator's confidentiality regime, with the timeline for mitigation and the escalation position.
- **Third-party evaluator variant.** Publish the specific reproduction under the evaluation agreement; the evaluator may be asked to test the proposed mitigation.
- **Board variant.** Publish the failure category, the mitigation timeline, and the escalation position; the specific reproduction is not what the board needs.

**The rationale.** Coordinated disclosure discipline (CVE-style) applies to safety failures too. Chapter `07` returns to the timelines in the audience-variant contract.

## The redaction manifest as a first-class artefact

Every non-public variant's head carries a `redaction_manifest`:

```yaml
redaction_manifest:
  variant: "public"                # this is what the public variant claims to *be* the canonical view
  # For non-public variants (regulator, third-party, board):
  # omitted_fields:
  #   - path: "safety.attack_payloads"
  #     omitted_from: "public"
  #     present_in: ["regulator", "third-party"]
  #     reason: "operational-security"
  #     rationale_content_address: "sha256:aaaa..."
  #   - path: "quality_attributes[attribute=societal-and-ethical-risk-mitigation].metrics[metric_family=iso-25059:privacy].raw_examples"
  #     omitted_from: "public"
  #     present_in: ["regulator", "third-party"]
  #     reason: "personal-data"
  #     rationale_content_address: "sha256:bbbb..."
  #   - path: "provenance.c2pa.canaries"
  #     omitted_from: "public"
  #     present_in: ["regulator", "third-party"]
  #     reason: "detection-mechanism-secrecy"
  #     rationale_content_address: "sha256:cccc..."
```

Two invariants:

- **The redaction is enumerated.** Every field withheld in a variant appears in the manifest. Silent redaction is broken.
- **The rationale is cited.** Each redaction row points at a rationale document. "We redacted because we chose to" is not a rationale; "we redacted attack payloads under program-policy `redaction-policy-v2` clause 4.2 to serve the operational-security exemption" is.

Chapter `07` operationalises the derivation: the four audience variants are computed from the canonical case by applying variant-specific `redaction_manifest`s. The redaction manifest is a first-class artefact — it too is content-addressed and signed.

## Interaction with the assurance case (mod-102) and the evidence store (mod-104)

Both C2PA and the redaction manifest have to be walkable inside the case + store shape.

- **C2PA manifests are evidence.** The C2PA manifest for a GenAI-output disclosure discharges an assurance-case goal (`goal:G1.S1.G-eu-ai-act-50-provenance` or an equivalent depending on jurisdiction). The manifest is a content-addressed artefact in the store, signed by the C2PA producer credential. Chapter `04` of mod-104 (supply-chain provenance) is where the credential-chain-of-trust lives.
- **The redaction manifest is evidence.** The redaction manifest discharges the case goal that "the audience-variant is faithful to the canonical case" (`goal:G1.S3.G-audience-variant-faithful`). It is content-addressed in the store, signed by the card producer.

Both artefacts survive the audit walks. A reviewer under EU AI Act Article 74 fetching the C2PA manifest walks the same six-step content-address verification any other evidence node uses.

## Anti-patterns

- **C2PA-without-a-manifest-in-the-store.** Shipping a signed manifest on the artefact but never storing it makes the manifest transient; six months later the card cites a manifest nobody can verify.
- **Watermarking-without-detector.** Advertising a watermarking scheme without publishing (or making available under regulatory access) the detection endpoint reduces the scheme to marketing.
- **Silent redaction.** Removing a field from the public variant without an entry in the `redaction_manifest`. A reviewer who compares public and regulator variants and finds a delta the manifest does not name has discovered a defeater.
- **Redaction-without-rationale.** Every redaction cites a policy row. Ad-hoc redaction breaks between releases.
- **Fake trade-secret cover.** Redacting a benchmark result as "confidential" when no confidentiality regime applies is misrepresentation. The reader (regulator or otherwise) will ask what regime justifies the redaction.
- **Publishing canaries.** Chapter `05` of mod-104 named the failure mode; a card that publishes decontamination canary tokens has just handed contamination-friendly training data pipelines the exact strings to filter out.

## Summary

- Every GenAI-output-producing system's assurance card carries a `provenance.c2pa` block that binds an in-store C2PA manifest, a producer credential, the specific assertions, and a `disclosure_position` reading to EU AI Act Article 50.
- Watermarking and IPTC-style provenance are complementary; the card can carry all three (C2PA, watermarking, IPTC-fallback) without them contradicting.
- The disclosure-vs-secrecy trade-off centres on five recurring fields: attack payloads, PII in eval sets, decontamination canaries, trade-secret / IP-protected training-data disclosures, and vulnerability windows.
- Each redaction is documented in a `redaction_manifest` — a first-class content-addressed artefact — with a cited rationale row. Silent redaction is broken.
- Both C2PA manifests and redaction manifests are evidence nodes in the store and discharge specific assurance-case goals.
- Chapter `07` operationalises the four audience variants as a derivation from the canonical case using the `redaction_manifest`.
